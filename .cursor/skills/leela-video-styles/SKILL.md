# Leela video styles

Read `style.md` first. Then pick **one** style from this list for the film. Say which style you picked and why before you build.

The voice is one continuous read. The picture keeps moving for the whole sentence. Captions sit in one lower-third place, two or three words at a time, and hand off. They are not a new card every phrase.

## Showreel prompt

Use this when the job is a style study or a résumé reel. Replace `x` with one name from the list. Do not mix styles inside that one film.

make a dynamic 15-second motion graphics video that shows what an incredible motion designer you are, like it's your showreel for a résumé. go all out. Do it in the style of “x”

## How to choose

1. Name the job: teach a fact, tell a story, launch a product, or show code.
2. Pick the style whose mood matches that job. If the last film on the site already used it, pick another.
3. Take two or three motion moves that belong to that style (a slam, a wipe, a fold, a glitch hit). Do not fall back to fade-in title cards.
4. Time those moves to the spoken words. One voice track. No second voice on top.

| Job | Start here | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| A fact with a diagram | Infographic, Blueprint, Swiss style, Line art | Glitch, Graffiti, Psychedelic |
| Code or a trace | ASCII art, Pixel art, Sci-fi HUD, Blueprint | Watercolor, Cross-stitch |
| A fable or character | Paper collage, Pencil sketch, Cel shading, Manga, Origami | Neumorphism, Bento grid |
| A product or launch | Kinetic type, Swiss style, Bento grid, Neon sign | Stained glass, Woodcut |
| A loud social reel | Pop art, Graffiti, Glitch, Y2K chrome | Blueprint, Film noir |
| A calm lesson | Flat 2D, Mid-century, Risograph, Topographic | Synthwave, Particles |

## The 50 styles

**Swiss style.** Grid, one sans, black and red, lots of empty space. Use for a launch or a fact that must be read instantly. Move type on the grid, not off it. Skip it for stories and characters.

**Kinetic type.** The words are the picture. They slam, track, and exit on the voice. Use for a manifesto, a rule, or a title. Skip it when the lesson needs a diagram.

**Pop art.** Ben-Day dots, thick black outlines, primary colours. Use for a joke, a product hit, or a repeated word. One colour per beat. Skip it for calm science.

**Flat 2D.** Solid shapes, no shading, simple icons. Use for a first explainer a child could follow. Shapes slide and swap. Skip it when the film needs texture or depth.

**Clay 3D.** Soft rounded forms, fingerprints, studio light. Use for a mascot or a toy, including a lattu. The object squashes and settles. Skip it for code and data.

**Glassmorphism.** Frosted panels, blur, thin light edges. Use for a product UI or a settings idea. Panels slide over a quiet ground. Skip it on busy footage.

**Y2K chrome.** Liquid metal, stars, bubble type, aqua and silver. Use for a playful tech promo. Reflections travel. Skip it for teaching and for weddings.

**Synthwave.** Horizon grid, sun, magenta and electric blue. Use for a night drive or a retro future sting. The grid rushes toward camera. Skip it for paper lessons.

**Risograph.** Misregistered ink, limited colours, paper grain. Use for a poster-like explainer or a chapter title. Layers drift a few pixels. Skip it for precise UI.

**Paper collage.** Cut paper, tape, torn edges. Use for a story, a memory, or a handmade lesson. Pieces drop in and can be pulled away. Skip it for code.

**Glitch.** A short RGB split or slice, then a clean frame. Use as a hit on a word, not as the whole film. Skip it for anything that must stay calm.

**Particles.** Dots that gather into a form, then release. Use for "many become one" (names, stars, a crowd). Skip it when the viewer must read a sentence in the same space.

**Neobrutalism.** Hard shadow, thick border, flat bright fill. Use for a bold product card or a sticker label. Cards stamp down. Skip it for film-noir or paper.

**Bauhaus.** Circle, square, triangle, primary colours, strict alignment. Use for a principle or a logo build. Shapes assemble into the mark. Skip it for texture-heavy stories.

**Memphis.** Squiggles, confetti shapes, pastel clash. Use for a playful list or a party reel. Shapes bounce in on the beat of the sentence. Skip it for serious science.

**Vaporwave.** Statues, palms, pink grid, slowed type. Use for a nostalgic internet joke. Hold the image and let type drift. Skip it for a lesson.

**Pixel art.** Chunk pixels, limited palette, step animation. Use for a game-like code story or a retro lab. Motion is on the pixel grid. Skip it for photography.

**Isometric.** 45-degree boxes, a small world you can orbit. Use for a building, a board, or a machine. The camera trucks, the world stays. Skip it for portraits.

**Low poly.** Faceted shapes, flat shaded planes. Use for a landscape or a simple object turning. Rotate the form. Skip it for fine type.

**ASCII art.** Characters as tone and picture. Use for a terminal, a trace, or a code lab. Glyphs resolve into the diagram. Skip it for emotion and for children.

**Frutiger Aero.** Glossy bubbles, water, sky, skeuomorphic shine from the 2000s. Use for a hopeful tech daydream. Highlights slide. Skip it for noir and for print looks.

**Op art.** Black and white lines that seem to move. Use for a tension beat or a "look closer" moment. The pattern shifts, then stops so type can be read. Skip it under body text.

**Cel shading.** Toon outlines, flat colour steps, like animation cels. Use for an original character who acts. The character enters, does one thing, exits. Skip it for charts.

**Art Deco.** Fans, gold lines, symmetry, tall type. Use for a title, a wedding, or a formal ending. Lines draw on, then the word lands. Skip it for messy or comic beats.

**Neumorphism.** Soft extruded controls on a same-colour ground. Use only for a single UI object. It fails contrast if the whole frame is soft. Skip it for type-heavy films.

**Stained glass.** Black leading, jewel colours, light through panes. Use for a moral, a fable ending, or a still metaphor. Panes light one by one. Skip it for UI and code.

**Film noir.** Black, one hard light, rain, long shadow. Use for a mystery or a problem before the answer. The light finds the subject. Skip it for cheerful products.

**Aurora gradient.** Slow colour fields, dark ground, soft glow. Use behind a quiet line, never as the only idea. The field drifts. Skip it when the website itself is already gray and the film would vanish into it.

**Blueprint.** White lines on blue, dimensions, stamps. Use for a plan, an architecture, or "how it is built". Lines draw in measured time. Skip it for emotion.

**Graffiti.** Spray fill, drip, a wall. Use for a street reel or a name shout. The fill rushes on, then the word is readable. Skip it for lessons and for weddings.

**Split-flap.** Airport board flaps that settle on letters. Use for a schedule, a list, or a departure. Each flap lands on a word of the voice. Skip it for drawings.

**Watercolor.** Bloom, paper, soft edges. Use for a feeling, a season, or a children's beat. Colour arrives as a wash. Skip it for numbers and code.

**Sci-fi HUD.** Thin frames, ticks, one data readout. Use for a scan, a trace, or a system status. Readouts update on the spoken fact. Skip it for handmade stories.

**Origami.** Folds, creases, a sheet becoming an object. Use for a transformation (an idea becoming a film, paper becoming a bird). The fold is the motion. Skip it for photoreal scenes.

**Manga.** Speed lines, panel borders, a held reaction. Use for a beat of surprise in a story. One panel, then the next. Skip it for dashboards.

**Psychedelic.** Melting colour, radial pulse. Use for a single dream beat. Pull back to a readable frame before any fact. Skip it for the whole lesson.

**Infographic.** One number, one icon, one comparison. Use when the film teaches. The icon arrives as the noun is said. Skip it for mood pieces.

**Woodcut.** Gouged black shapes, rough paper. Use for a fable or an old rule. The shape stamps down. Skip it for modern UI.

**Neon sign.** Dark wall, tube light that flickers on, then holds. Use for a name, a place, or a night title. The tube draws itself. Skip it in daylight scenes.

**Mid-century.** Teardrop shapes, mustard, olive, walnut. Use for a home, a chair, or a calm product. Shapes swing in on an arc. Skip it for cyber and code.

**Topographic.** Contour lines that tighten around a point. Use for a map, a search, or "closer". The lines converge as the answer is named. Skip it for faces.

**Cross-stitch.** A grid of stitches that fill an icon. Use for a craft, a gift, or a patient build. Stitches fill on the words. Skip it for speed and for UI.

**Skeuomorphism.** A real-feeling object: paper, metal, a clapper. Use for one prop the hand would touch. It lands with weight. Skip it as a whole-frame texture.

**Tilt-shift.** A miniature look, shallow focus. Use for a city, a crowd, or a table of objects. Focus pulls to the one thing that matters. Skip it for flat graphics.

**Pencil sketch.** Construction lines, then the drawing firms up. Use for "figuring it out" before the answer. Lines complete as the sentence completes. Skip it for final UI.

**Art Nouveau.** Whiplash curves, floral frames, tall lettering. Use for a name or a nature title. The vine draws, then the word. Skip it for data.

**Bento grid.** Uneven tiles, each tile one fact. Use for a feature list or a product tour. Tiles fill in an order the voice names. Skip it for a single emotional shot.

**Grunge.** Scratches, stamps, worn type. Use for a rough cut or a protest line. One stamp, then clean type so it can be read. Skip it for a gentle story.

**Surrealism.** One impossible join (a door in the sky, a clock melting once). Use for a metaphor, then return to the plain idea. Skip it when the fact must be literal.

**Line art.** One stroke weight, no fill, the drawing is the motion. Use for a how-to or a path. The line draws in the order of the sentence. Skip it when colour is the point.
